# NAS 2009 Forensic Science Critique and FDE Reliability Research

**Sources**:
- National Research Council (2009). Strengthening Forensic Science in the United States: A Path Forward
- Kam et al. (1994, 1997). Proficiency Studies and Error Rate Research
- Sita et al. (2002). Expertise and Error Rate Studies

## Overview

The 2009 National Academy of Sciences report "Strengthening Forensic Science in the United States: A Path Forward" was a landmark critique of forensic science practices, including forensic document examination. This reference addresses the scientific validity concerns, error rate research, and ongoing developments in FDE reliability.

## Core Principles

### 1. Scientific Validity Assessment
- All forensic sciences require validated methodologies
- Error rates must be known and documented
- Peer review and publication of methods is essential
- Proficiency testing must be regular and meaningful

### 2. Transparency and Standards
- Limitations of methods must be acknowledged
- Uncertainty must be quantified and communicated
- Standardization of terminology and procedures is needed
- Research foundation must be accessible and critiquable

### 3. Quality and Improvement
- Ongoing research and development is essential
- Training and certification standards must be rigorous
- Quality assurance programs must be systematic
- Field must respond to criticism with improvement

## NAS 2009 Key Findings Relevant to FDE

### Scientific Validity Concerns

**1. Methodology Validation**
- **NAS Finding**: Many forensic disciplines lack validated methodologies
- **FDE Context**: Document examination has established standards (ASTM, SWGDOC) but limited published validation studies
- **Ongoing Response**: Increased emphasis on empirical validation research

**2. Error Rate Documentation**
- **NAS Finding**: Error rates are often unknown or poorly documented
- **FDE Context**: Several empirical studies exist but limited comprehensive error rate data
- **Ongoing Response**: Expanded proficiency testing and error rate research

**3. Peer Review Publication**
- **NAS Finding**: Limited peer-reviewed research in many forensic disciplines
- **FDE Context**: Growing body of published research but gaps remain
- **Ongoing Response**: Increased publication in peer-reviewed journals

### Reliability Assessment Framework

**1. Foundational Requirements**
- **Validated Methodology**: Methods must be tested and shown to be reliable
- **Known Error Rates**: False positive and false negative rates must be documented
- **Standards Development**: Professional standards must be systematically developed
- **Proficiency Testing**: Regular testing with meaningful feedback

**2. Evidentiary Standards**
- **Daubert Criteria**: Scientific validity, error rates, peer review, general acceptance
- **Joiner/Kumho Extensions**: All expert testimony subject to reliability assessment
- **FDE Response**: Development of standards and research to meet Daubert criteria

**3. Research Foundation**
- **Empirical Studies**: Studies on examiner accuracy and error rates
- **Basic Research**: Research on handwriting individuality and feature analysis
- **Methodological Research**: Studies on comparison methods and their validity
- **Critical Research**: Research examining limitations and potential errors

## Empirical Error Rate Research

### Key Proficiency Studies

**Kam, Wetstein, & Conn (1994)**
- **Study Design**: 36 professional document examiners tested on signature cases
- **Results**: 97% correct identification rate, 2.4% false positive rate, 0.6% false negative rate
- **Significance**: First major empirical study of FDE examiner accuracy
- **Limitations**: Specific conditions, limited sample size, controlled environment

**Kam, Fielding, & Conn (1997)**
- **Study Design**: Writer identification with professional examiners
- **Results**: 94% correct rate, error rates varying by difficulty
- **Significance**: Confirmed and extended earlier findings
- **Limitations**: Controlled conditions, professional examiners only

**Sita, Found, & Rogers (2002)**
- **Study Design**: Signature comparison with expert examiners
- **Results**: High accuracy rates, expertise correlated with performance
- **Significance**: Demonstrated expertise effects and validated professional approach
- **Limitations**: Expert sample may not represent all examiners

### Error Rate Context

**Documented Error Rates**:
- **False Positives**: 0.6% - 3% (incorrect identification)
- **False Negatives**: 0.6% - 6% (missed identifications)
- **Overall Accuracy**: 94% - 97% in empirical studies

**Error Context Factors**:
- **Case Difficulty**: Error rates increase with difficult comparisons
- **Sample Quality**: Poor quality samples increase error rates
- **Examiner Experience**: Experienced examiners show lower error rates
- **Methodological Rigor**: Systematic approach reduces errors

**Limitations of Error Rate Research**:
- **Controlled Conditions**: Most studies use controlled conditions
- **Professional Examiners**: Studies typically test experienced professionals
- **Limited Scope**: Studies cover specific types of cases
- **Real-World Gaps**: Limited data on actual casework error rates

- **Ongoing Development**: Continued standards development for new methods

**2. SWGDOC Standards**

- **Methodology Documentation**: Standardized approaches to examination
- **Quality Assurance**: Standards for quality programs and proficiency testing

## Legal Standards and Challenges

### Daubert Criteria and FDE

**1. Scientific Validity**
- **Criterion**: Methodology must be scientifically valid
- **FDE Status**: Established methodology with ongoing validation research
- **Challenges**: Limited published validation studies for some methods
- **Response**: Increased emphasis on empirical validation

**2. Known Error Rates**
- **Criterion**: Error rates must be known and available
- **FDE Status**: Error rates documented in empirical studies
- **Challenges**: Limited comprehensive error rate data
- **Response**: Expanded proficiency testing and research

**3. Peer Review and Publication**
- **Criterion**: Methods must be subject to peer review
- **FDE Status**: Growing body of peer-reviewed research
- **Challenges**: Historical lack of published research
- **Response**: Increased publication in peer-reviewed outlets

**4. General Acceptance**
- **Criterion**: Methods must be generally accepted in the field
- **FDE Status**: Long-standing acceptance in courts
- **Challenges**: Acceptance not sufficient post-Daubert
- **Response**: Focus on scientific validation beyond acceptance

### Evidentiary Challenges

**1. Admissibility Challenges**
- **Frequency**: Increased challenges post-NAS report
- **Focus**: Scientific validity and error rates
- **Success**: Mixed results in admissibility challenges
- **Trend**: Courts increasingly demanding scientific foundation

**2. Expert Testimony Standards**
- **Qualification**: Stricter standards for expert qualification
- **Methodology**: Detailed explanation of methods required
- **Limitations**: Explicit discussion of limitations expected
- **Uncertainty**: Quantification of uncertainty increasingly required

**3. Professional Response**
- **Standards Development**: Systematic development of professional standards
- **Research Expansion**: Increased emphasis on empirical research
- **Quality Programs**: More rigorous quality assurance programs
- **Transparency**: Greater openness about limitations and uncertainty

## Practical Implications

### For Legal Professionals

**1. Understanding Reliability**
- FDE has empirical support but limitations exist
- Error rates are documented but not comprehensive
- Methods are systematic but not all are validated
- Examiners vary in expertise and approach

**2. Effective Cross-Examination**
- Focus on specific methodology used in case
- Question error rate applicability to case facts
- Examine quality assurance and proficiency testing
- Address limitations and alternative explanations

**3. Case Assessment**

- Assess appropriateness of methodology to case
- Understand limitations and their implications
